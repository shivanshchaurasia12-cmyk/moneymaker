How to Read a Candlestick Chart: A Beginner Guide for TradingView

By the Money Moves India editorial team · Published 1 October 2026 · 4 min read

A price chart can look like noise on day one. It stops looking like noise once you can read a single candle. This guide covers just that, with a practice routine at the end. It applies to TradingView and to most other charting platforms.

One candle, four prices

Every candle summarises one period of time. If your chart is set to "1 day", each candle is one day. If it is set to "15 minutes", each candle is fifteen minutes. It records four prices from that period: the open, the high, the low and the close.

The thick part is the body. It runs from the open to the close. The thin lines above and below are the wicks (also called shadows). They show how far the price travelled beyond the body before coming back.

Reading the colours

On most platforms, including TradingView by default, green means the price closed higher than it opened and red means it closed lower. Colours can be changed in the settings, so check which colour your chart uses before reading anything into it.

What the shapes can tell you

ShapeWhat happened in that periodCareful reading
Long body, tiny wicksPrice moved firmly one way from open to close.Strong move, but it says nothing about the next candle.
Small body, long wicksPrice went far in both directions, then returned near its open.Indecision. Buyers and sellers both pushed and neither won.
Long lower wickSellers pushed the price down, then buyers pushed it back up.Shows rejection of lower prices in that period only.
Long upper wickBuyers pushed the price up, then sellers pushed it back down.Shows rejection of higher prices in that period only.

None of these shapes predicts the future. They describe what already happened, and that is why they are useful for learning and not a promise of what comes next.

Timeframes change the story

The same stock can look like a steady climb on a weekly chart and a messy zigzag on a 5-minute chart. Both are true. Beginners often get stuck switching timeframes until they find one that matches what they hoped to see. Pick one timeframe, such as daily, and learn it properly first. You will find the timeframe selector in the toolbar at the top of the chart.

Indicators come later

Tools like moving averages, RSI and MACD are calculations built from price. They do not add new information. Adding five of them to a chart you cannot yet read just creates colourful clutter. Learn candles first. Then add one indicator at a time, and write down what it is supposed to tell you.

Three practice exercises

  1. Label ten candles. Pick any stock or index on a daily chart. For ten candles in a row, write down the open, high, low and close and say whether it was green or red.
  2. Find the wicks. Scroll back three months. Mark five candles with a long upper or lower wick. What did the next three candles do? Notice that the answer varies.
  3. Switch timeframes. Look at the same week on a daily chart and on a 1-hour chart. Write one sentence about what each chart makes easier to see.

Do these on past data. Once you are comfortable, practise with a paper-trading account before you put real money anywhere near a chart. Our article on revenge trading explains why that order matters.

Mistakes beginners make with candles

  • Reading one candle as a forecast. A long green candle does not mean tomorrow will be green. Candles describe a finished period.
  • Memorising dozens of named patterns. Learn what each part of a candle means first. Names are labels for shapes you will recognise on your own.
  • Ignoring volume and context. The same shape means different things after a long fall than after a long rise. Always look at what came before.
  • Zooming in until everything looks dramatic. A small chart range makes tiny moves look huge. Check the price scale on the right side.
  • Trading from the chart before practising. Reading is a skill. Give it weeks, not days.

A short glossary

WordMeaning
BodyThe thick part of the candle between open and close
Wick or shadowThe thin line above or below the body
TimeframeHow much time one candle represents
BullishDescribes price moving up, or a view that it will
BearishDescribes price moving down, or a view that it will
VolumeHow much of the instrument was traded in the period

Check your understanding

Look at this description: open 250, high 262, low 248, close 259. Which colour is the candle, how long is the upper wick, and how long is the lower wick? The close is above the open, so the candle is green (on default settings). The upper wick runs from 259 up to 262, which is 3 points. The lower wick runs from 250 down to 248, which is 2 points. If you got all three, you can already read the most important parts of a chart.
