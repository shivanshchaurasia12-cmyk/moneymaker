Leverage in Trading: What a 1% Move Does to a Rs 20,000 Account

By the Money Moves India editorial team · Published 1 October 2026 · 4 min read

Many beginners read "leverage up to 1:100" as a bonus. It is closer to a magnifying glass. It makes your profits bigger and your losses bigger by the same factor, and the loss side is the one that ends accounts.

Before the example, let us get the four words straight, because they are often mixed up.

The four terms in plain English

TermWhat it meansExample
PipThe usual smallest price step in a currency pair. For most pairs it is the fourth decimal place.EUR/USD moving from 1.1000 to 1.1001 is one pip.
LotThe size of a position. A standard lot is 100,000 units of the base currency. A mini lot is 10,000 and a micro lot is 1,000.Buying 1 micro lot of EUR/USD means 1,000 euros of exposure.
LeverageHow many times bigger your position is than the money you put up.At 1:20, Rs 1 of your money controls Rs 20 of market exposure.
MarginThe part of your own money that is locked to hold the position.Rs 5,000 margin at 1:20 holds a Rs 1,00,000 position.

One thing trips people up: margin is not the most you can lose. It is only the deposit your broker asks for. The loss is worked out on the full position.

What a pip is worth

For EUR/USD, one pip on a standard lot is about 10 US dollars. On a micro lot it is about 10 cents. So a 30-pip move on one micro lot is roughly 3 dollars. Pairs that are not quoted in dollars need a currency conversion, so use your provider's calculator instead of guessing.

A worked example: same market, four different positions

Take an account with Rs 20,000. A trader opens a position, and the price moves 1% against it. The only thing that changes below is leverage. These figures are illustrative and use simple maths. They ignore fees and spreads.

Leverage usedPosition sizeLoss from a 1% adverse moveShare of the account lost
5xRs 1,00,000Rs 1,0005%
20xRs 4,00,000Rs 4,00020%
50xRs 10,00,000Rs 10,00050%
100xRs 20,00,000Rs 20,000100%

At 5x, a bad move is annoying. At 100x, the same 1% move erases the whole account. The market did nothing different. Only the position size changed.

In real trading the broker usually closes your position automatically once your balance gets too low (often called a stop-out). So you may not lose a full 100%, but that is only because the money has run out, not because you were protected.

Work out the risk first, then the size

A calmer way to trade is to decide how much you are willing to lose before you pick a position size.

  1. Pick a risk limit per trade. Many beginners practise with 1% of the account. On Rs 20,000 that is Rs 200.
  2. Decide where the trade is proven wrong (your stop-loss level) and how far that is from your entry. Say it is 1%.
  3. Position size = money at risk ÷ distance to stop = Rs 200 ÷ 0.01 = Rs 20,000.

A Rs 20,000 position on a Rs 20,000 account is 1x leverage. In this case you did not need borrowed exposure at all. If your stop is closer, the maths allows a bigger position, but the loss if you are wrong is still Rs 200. That is the real purpose of position sizing.

Three leverage mistakes beginners make

  1. Treating margin as the maximum loss. If you put Rs 5,000 as margin on a Rs 1,00,000 position, a 5% fall costs Rs 5,000, which is your whole margin. A 10% fall costs Rs 10,000, which is more than you deposited as margin. The loss follows the position, not the deposit.
  2. Using the highest leverage on offer. A broker allowing 1:100 does not mean 1:100 is sensible for you. The setting is a limit, not a target.
  3. Adding to a losing position. Buying more because the price is "cheaper now" increases the size when your idea is currently being proven wrong. That is revenge trading in a polite disguise. We cover it in our guide to revenge trading.

Try it yourself

Practise with your own numbers before you touch any platform. Say you have Rs 30,000, you risk 1% per trade, and your stop-loss would sit 2% away from your entry price.

StepCalculationResult
Money you accept losingRs 30,000 × 1%Rs 300
Distance to your stop2% of the price0.02
Position sizeRs 300 ÷ 0.02Rs 15,000
Leverage neededRs 15,000 ÷ Rs 30,0000.5x (none)

The answer is that you do not need leverage at all. If the maths of your plan says you need 20x to make a trade worthwhile, the plan may be asking for more risk than your account can carry. Change the plan, not the leverage.

Where to verify this

Rules, fees and menus change. Check the latest version at:

  • Your broker's contract specification page for the exact pip value, lot size and margin rules of each product.
  • The Reserve Bank of India website (rbi.org.in) for current rules on forex trading by residents of India.
