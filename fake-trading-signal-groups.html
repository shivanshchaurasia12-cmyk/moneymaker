8 Warning Signs of a Fake Trading Signal Group

By the Money Moves India editorial team · Published 1 October 2026 · 4 min read

Open any social platform and you will find groups offering "100% accurate signals" and "daily profit". Some are just unskilled. Others are built to take your money. The first step to staying safe is to know the common patterns.

A quick word on what a signal is

A signal is someone's suggestion to buy or sell something at a certain price. Even a genuine, skilled analyst is wrong regularly. If a signal arrives without the size, the exit and the reason, you cannot judge it. And if you cannot judge it, you are guessing with their confidence.

Eight warning signs

Warning signWhy it mattersWhat to check
Guaranteed or fixed returnsNo honest market participant can promise a result.Treat it as a scam until proven otherwise.
Screenshots as proofA screenshot can be edited, or can show one win and hide ten losses.Ask for a full, dated record with losing trades included.
Pressure to deposit nowUrgency stops you from thinking and checking.Take 48 hours. A real opportunity will still exist.
Paying into a personal accountA company handling money should use a company account.Never send money to an individual's UPI ID or account for trading.
Unknown platformYou may be depositing into a site that is not allowed for Indian residents or does not return money.Look for the company name, address and regulator.
No adviser registrationGiving paid investment advice generally needs registration with the regulator.Search the person or firm on the SEBI website.
Fake testimonials and invented profitComments can be written by the group's own accounts.Look for independent, verifiable reviews, not comments inside the group.
Requests for login details or OTPsAnyone with your OTP can move your money.Never share OTPs, passwords or screen access.

A typical scam sequence

It often begins with a free group and a few correct-looking calls. After some days, an "exclusive" paid group or a "special account" appears, and the admin suggests a platform with a high bonus. A small first withdrawal works so you build trust. Then you are asked to deposit more, and later you are told you must pay "tax" or a "release fee" to take out your balance. That final fee is the tell. It is a request for more money, not a charge.

What to do instead

  • Learn the basics first. Our guide to leverage and position size shows how quickly a leveraged position can go wrong.
  • Practise on paper before using real money.
  • Use only registered brokers and read our legality checklist.
  • If a stranger's alerts feel necessary, you have not yet learned enough to trade independently.

If you have already lost money

Stop sending more. Save screenshots, account numbers, chat logs and receipts. Tell your bank immediately and report the case on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal or by calling 1930. Do not pay anyone who offers to "recover" your money for a fee. Recovery scams target the same victims.

How a genuine educator behaves differently

Not everyone who shares market ideas is a scammer. Some people teach honestly. The difference shows up in how they handle uncertainty and money.

Genuine educatorScam group
Says losses are normal and shows themShows only wins
Teaches you how to analyse so you can decideTells you what to copy
Explains position size and exit before the tradePosts only 'buy now' messages
Is registered, or says clearly they are not giving adviceNever mentions registration
Takes payment for education through a clear businessTakes money to personal accounts
Lets you leave without pressurePushes you to deposit more or 'upgrade'

Questions to ask before joining anything

  • What is your registration number, and which authority issued it?
  • Can I see a complete, dated record of every call, including the losing ones?
  • What do I pay, to whom, and what exactly do I get?
  • What happens to my money if I want to stop?

A reasonable person answers these without getting angry. A scammer dodges, flatters or tells you the offer is about to close.

Protecting people around you

Scam groups often spread through friends and family. If someone you know is being pulled in, do not shame them. Ask them to try the questions above, and offer to look at the platform together.

Where to verify this

Rules, fees and menus change. Check the latest version at:

  • SEBI's website to check whether someone giving investment advice is a registered adviser.
  • RBI's alert list of unauthorised forex trading platforms.
  • The National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in) if you have already lost money to a scam.
