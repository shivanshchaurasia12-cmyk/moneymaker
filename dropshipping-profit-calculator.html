Dropshipping Profit Maths: Where the Money in a Rs 699 Order Actually Goes

By the Money Moves India editorial team · Published 1 October 2026 · 4 min read

The usual dropshipping pitch goes like this: buy a product for Rs 300, sell it for Rs 699, keep Rs 399. It sounds easy because it skips every cost that appears after the sale. Let us put the missing costs back in.

One order, line by line

These figures are an example, not a quote. Your supplier, courier and gateway will charge different amounts.

ItemAmountNote
Selling priceRs 699What the customer pays
Supplier costRs 300What you pay the supplier
ShippingRs 70Charged by the courier
Payment fee (about 2%)Rs 14Gateway charge on Rs 699
Returns allowanceRs 60See below
Left before advertisingRs 255699 − 300 − 70 − 14 − 60

About the returns allowance. Suppose 15% of orders come back or are refunded, and each one costs you about Rs 400 in lost shipping and handling. Spread across every order, that is roughly Rs 60. If your product is clothing or footwear, your return rate can be higher, so measure it instead of copying our number.

The number that decides everything: break-even ad cost

After those costs, Rs 255 is left from each order. That is the most you can spend on advertising to get one order and still break even.

If it costs you this much to win one orderYou make or lose per order
Rs 150Profit of Rs 105
Rs 250Profit of Rs 5
Rs 350Loss of Rs 95

Notice how quickly it flips. A store that looks like it has a Rs 399 margin can lose money on every sale once advertising goes above Rs 255. And this still ignores GST, your time and software subscriptions.

What to do with this

  1. Build your own version of the table in a spreadsheet before you list a product.
  2. Ask the supplier for the real delivery time and the return process. Slow delivery drives refunds.
  3. Order one sample yourself. Check the quality and the packaging you would be sending to customers.
  4. Test with a small ad budget and track the cost per order every day.
  5. Only scale when cost per order stays comfortably below your break-even number.

It is not passive income

Dropshipping saves you from holding stock. It does not save you from finding products, handling customer messages, dealing with failed deliveries or keeping records. Treat it as a small business that happens to have no warehouse.

Four things that quietly make the numbers worse

  • Cash on delivery. If a customer refuses the parcel, you may pay shipping both ways and the product never gets sold. Track your refusal rate separately from normal returns.
  • Price comparison. Customers can search for your product, find the same item for less, and ask for a refund. Be sure your price still works when a rival drops theirs.
  • GST. If you must register for GST, part of the Rs 699 belongs to the government, not to you. The rules depend on your turnover and what you sell, so check the GST portal or ask an accountant.
  • Your own time. If an order costs you ten minutes of messages and follow-ups, add that to the cost before you celebrate a "profit" of Rs 5.

A seven-day test plan

Do not spend a large budget on day one. Treat the first week as an experiment that answers a single question: can this product make money after every cost?

DayWhat to doWhat you are learning
1Fill in the cost table for the product. Include a guess for returns.The break-even ad cost per order
2Order one sample for yourself.Real quality, real delivery time
3Write the listing in your own words and take at least one honest photo.Whether the product is as described
4 to 6Run a very small ad budget you can afford to lose entirely.Cost to win one order
7Compare actual cost per order with your break-even number.Continue, change the product or stop

If you stop after a week with a small, planned loss and a clear answer, that is a good outcome. It is far cheaper than finding the answer after three months of spending.

Where to verify this

Rules, fees and menus change. Check the latest version at:

  • The fee pages of your payment gateway and shipping partner, since both change.
  • The GST rules on the Government of India GST portal (gst.gov.in) for whether you need to register and charge GST.
