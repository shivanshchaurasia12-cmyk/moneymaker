Build a Student Expense Tracker in Google Sheets in 20 Minutes

By the Money Moves India editorial team · Published 1 October 2026 · 4 min read

You do not need a paid app to know where your pocket money or salary goes. A Google Sheet with two tabs is enough. This guide builds one, step by step. You will need a free Google account.

Step 1: Create the log sheet

  1. Open sheets.google.com and click Blank spreadsheet.
  2. Click the title at the top-left, type My Money Tracker and press Enter.
  3. Right-click the tab named "Sheet1" at the bottom, choose Rename and type Log.
  4. In row 1, type these headings: A1 Date, B1 What I bought, C1 Category, D1 Amount, E1 Paid by.

Step 2: Add a dropdown for Category

  1. Click the letter C at the top of column C to select the whole column.
  2. Click Data in the menu, then Data validation, then Add rule.
  3. Under Criteria choose Dropdown. Add four options: Fixed, Needs, Wants, Savings.
  4. Click Done.

What each category means:

CategoryPut these hereExample
FixedBills that repeat every monthPhone plan, hostel rent, subscriptions
NeedsThings you must buy, but amounts changeGroceries, bus pass, books
WantsNice to have, not requiredSnacks, games, new clothes
SavingsMoney you set asideRecurring deposit, emergency fund

Step 4: Make the summary tab

  1. Click the + at the bottom-left to add a new tab. Rename it Summary.
  2. In A1 type Category and in B1 type Total spent.
  3. In A2 to A5 type: Fixed, Needs, Wants, Savings.
  4. Click B2 and type this formula, then press Enter: =SUMIF(Log!C:C, A2, Log!D:D)
  5. Click B2 again, copy it (Ctrl+C), select B3 to B5 and paste (Ctrl+V).
  6. In A7 type Money received this month and type your amount in B7.
  7. In A8 type Money left. In B8 type: =B7-SUM(B2:B5)

With the sample rows above, the Summary tab shows Fixed 299, Needs 180, Wants 250 and Savings 500. If you received Rs 3,000 this month, "Money left" shows 1,771. Savings is counted as money set aside, so it reduces what is left to spend, which is what most students want.

Step 5: Build the habit

  • Add each expense the same day. Doing it later means you forget small items.
  • Pick one fixed time each week, for example Sunday evening, and spend ten minutes looking at the Summary tab.
  • If "Wants" is the largest number, decide on a weekly limit and write it in a cell beside it.
  • At month end, make a copy of the file (File, then Make a copy) so every month stays separate.

Keep it small

A tracker you keep updating beats a perfect one you abandon in week two. Resist adding more columns until the basic version feels natural. Three numbers matter most at the start: what came in, what went out, and what is left.

Reading your summary

After a few weeks, the Summary tab tells a story. Here is how to read it without judging yourself.

  • If Fixed is large, your money is committed before the month starts. Look for a subscription you forgot about.
  • If Needs swings a lot from week to week, plan a weekly amount and keep a small buffer.
  • If Wants is the biggest number, you do not need to remove it. Set a limit you can live with.
  • If Savings is zero, start with a small, regular amount. Rs 200 every week beats Rs 2,000 once.

Some people use a rough rule, such as spending half on needs, thirty percent on wants and saving the rest. Treat any rule like that as a starting point. Your own numbers are more useful than someone else's ratio.

Fixing common problems

ProblemLikely causeFix
The total shows 0Category spelling in the Summary tab does not match the dropdown, or the amount was typed as textMake sure A2 to A5 spell the categories exactly as in the dropdown, and type amounts as plain numbers with no Rs sign
Dropdown does not appearThe rule was added to a different columnSelect column C again and repeat Data, Data validation, Add rule
Formula shows an errorThe tab is not named exactly LogRename the tab to Log or edit the formula to match the tab name
Numbers include old monthsThe Log has several months of dataStart a new copy of the file each month, or add a month filter later once you are comfortable

Next steps once the basics work

When you have used the tracker for a month, try one upgrade at a time: a column for the month, a pie chart on the Summary tab (select the data, then Insert, then Chart), or a rule that turns the "Money left" cell red if it falls below a number you choose. Add a feature only when you feel a real need for it.

Where to verify this
